Wat Phra Mahathat Woramahawihan in Nakhon Si Thammarat officially earns UNESCO World Heritage status on July 25. 

Its towering white chedi, crowned with a gold spire you can spot from streets away, has anchored Thailand’s deep south for over 700 years – incense drifting, pilgrims circling, monks going about the day.

The nomination begins back in 2012, then grinds through 14-odd years of paperwork, site visits and community campaigning. Delegates at the 48th World Heritage Committee session in Busan vote it through unanimously. 

It’s the first cultural listing in all of Southern Thailand, the country’s sixth, its ninth site overall. For a region the glossy maps usually skip, that’s a proper win.
