Got plans this Sunday? Make room for Saturn. On October 4, the ringed planet reaches opposition, lining up with Earth and the Sun to sit at its closest point to us all year. It rises in the east around sunset and stays in view until dawn, weather permitting, so you can catch it whenever suits.

Spotting it is easy. Look for a steady, pale-gold dot that doesn’t twinkle like the stars nearby. It’s the farthest planet you can see with the naked eye, roughly 1.261 billion kilometres away. 

Want the rings? You’ll need a small telescope, but the good news is they’re at their brightest now after vanishing edge-on in 2025.
