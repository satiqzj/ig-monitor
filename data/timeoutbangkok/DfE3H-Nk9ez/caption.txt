Some recipes are too good to leave in the past. Meet hom ranjun, Khao San Sek’s (@khaosansek.bkk) take on gaeng ranjuan, a traditional Thai curry with a resourceful backstory. Founded by Chef Pam, who made history in 2025 as the first Asian and Thai female chef to receive the World’s Best Female Chef award, Khao San Sek brings a fresh perspective to this heritage dish.

Originally made by repurposing leftover nam prik kapi (shrimp-paste chilli relish), the dish turns familiar ingredients into a fragrant curry. At Khao San Sek, tender pork soft-bone ribs sit in a savoury broth with Thai herbs and sour, salty, sweet and spicy flavours.
Its roots lie in royal kitchens, but the thinking behind it is familiar to home cooks: make the most of what you have and let nothing good go to waste. Khao San Sek’s version keeps that culinary know-how at the table, introducing a new generation of diners to a lesser-known Thai recipe.

The dish has also earned the restaurant Classic Selection 2026 recognition through Thailand’s Best Local Food, under the concept ‘The Lost Taste: The Living Heritage’, celebrating the preservation of traditional recipes and food culture.

Khao San Sek is taking its next steps onto the international stage with ‘Khao San Sek’ set for Perth and ‘SEK’ for Sydney, the expansion is an opportunity to share more than just the flavours of Thailand. Each dish carries a story of local ingredients, culinary traditions and the cultural heritage that shapes Thai food. For Thai diners abroad, it offers a taste of home; for newcomers, a chance to discover a side of Thai cuisine that goes beyond the familiar orders.
